# E3 — Reliability and Failure of First-Order Principal-Subspace Risk Estimates

**Project:** Spectral Stability of Principal Subspaces in Equity Covariance Estimation  
**Status:** Pre-experiment protocol — draft v0.5 (not yet frozen)  
**Results:** None. All expected outcomes remain hypotheses.

## 1. Research question

**Under which spectral and sampling conditions does first-order perturbation theory accurately estimate expected principal-subspace error? How reliable is the estimate when the population covariance is unknown?**

The experiment addresses these questions through two comparisons:

- **E3-A — Oracle benchmark:** A first-order benchmark based on the *true population eigenvalues* is compared with expected subspace error. This tests the approximation itself.
- **E3-B — Sample-based benchmark:** The same formula is evaluated using *sample eigenvalues*. This tests its usefulness when only observed data are available.

The main goal is to map the **transition region (the “messy middle”)** where the benchmarks are neither clearly accurate nor clearly unreliable.

E3 builds on E2, which compared covariance spectra with the same boundary eigengap. E3 instead examines the accuracy of predicted estimation error. It does not test market-regime detection or trading performance.

## 2. Model and quantities

### Data and covariance

For each configuration, independent observations are drawn from

$$
X_1,\ldots,X_n\stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal N_d(0,\Sigma),
\qquad \Sigma\succ0.
$$

The centered, unbiased sample covariance is

$$
\widehat\Sigma=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^{n}
(X_r-\bar X)(X_r-\bar X)^\top.
$$

The population eigenvalues are denoted by $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d>0$, and the sample eigenvalues by $\widehat\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\widehat\lambda_d\ge0$. The projectors onto the leading $k$-dimensional subspaces are $P_k$ and $\widehat P_k$, respectively. Throughout, $k<d$ and the population boundary gap is positive:

$$
\delta_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}>0.
$$

### Realized loss and expected risk

The normalized loss for one sample is

$$
L_k=\frac{\|\widehat P_k-P_k\|_F^2}{2k}
=\frac1k\sum_{a=1}^{k}\sin^2\theta_a,
\qquad 0\le L_k\le1.
$$

Here, $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k$ are the principal angles between the population and estimated subspaces.

The **population risk** is the expected loss over repeated samples:

$$
R(\Sigma,n,k)=\mathbb E[L_k].
$$

The realized loss $L_k$ varies across samples. For fixed $(\Sigma,n,k)$, the risk $R$ is a single number. It is estimated by Monte Carlo simulation.

### Theoretical (oracle) benchmark

$$
B(\Sigma,n,k)=\frac{1}{k(n-1)}
\sum_{\substack{i\le k\\j>k}}
\frac{\lambda_i\lambda_j}{(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2}.
$$

This benchmark is the **first-order approximation** to expected squared projector error under the Gaussian model. It is not an exact finite-sample formula. In particular, $B$ can exceed 1 even though $R\le1$.

### Sample-based (plug-in) benchmark

$$
\widehat B=\frac{1}{k(n-1)}
\sum_{\substack{i\le k\\j>k}}
\frac{\widehat\lambda_i\widehat\lambda_j}
{(\widehat\lambda_i-\widehat\lambda_j)^2}.
$$

Unlike $B$, the plug-in estimate $\widehat B$ can be calculated from observed returns.

**Extreme-value warning.** A small sample eigengap can make $\widehat B$ very large. Under the full-rank real Gaussian covariance model used here, the raw estimator satisfies

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[\widehat B]=\infty.}
$$

Individual values are finite almost surely, but rare extreme values make the mathematical mean infinite. Therefore, the raw sample mean, mean absolute error, and RMSE are not suitable as primary performance measures. The main analysis instead uses medians, quantiles, and frequencies of large errors. A proof based on the real-Wishart eigenvalue density is outside the scope of this protocol.

A capped version is also considered:

$$
\widehat B_{\mathrm{cap}}=\min\{1,\max\{0,\widehat B\}\}.
$$

Capping keeps the estimate in the range of $R$. It is a practical adjustment, not a correction to perturbation theory. Since $R\in[0,1]$, capping cannot increase absolute error relative to $R$. This does not, by itself, establish that the estimate is more informative.

## 3. Controlled covariance families

The primary experiment fixes $d=12$ and $k=3$. Each population covariance is diagonal, with eigenvalues

$$
\lambda(\delta,t)=\left(
6,\ 4,\ 2.5+\frac\delta2,\ 2.5-\frac\delta2,\
\underbrace{1+t,\ldots,1+t}_{4},\
\underbrace{1-t,\ldots,1-t}_{4}
\right).
$$

The parameters satisfy $0<\delta\le1$ and $t\in\{0,0.8\}$. The top-3 boundary eigengap equals $\delta$. Across configurations, the population top-3 subspace, dimension, trace ($23$), and largest eigenvalue ($6$) remain fixed. Changing $t$ changes the remaining spectrum.

At $\delta=1$, the two spectra match those used in E2. Population eigenvectors are not rotated between configurations.

**Primary grid**

| Parameter | Values |
|---|---|
| Sample size $n$ | $50,100,250,500,1000,2000$ |
| Boundary gap $\delta$ | $0.05,0.10,0.20,0.35,0.50,0.75,1.00$ |
| Background spectrum $t$ | $0,0.8$ |
| **Total** | **84 configurations** |

The effect of each parameter is assessed while the others remain fixed. Results pooled across different sample sizes or background spectra will not be presented as effects of the eigengap alone.

Some small-sample, small-gap configurations are **clear breakdown examples**. For instance, $B>1$ is incompatible with an accurate absolute approximation to a risk bounded by 1. These cases provide reference points. The main focus is the transition region, where $B$ appears plausible but may still be inaccurate.

## 4. Hypotheses

**H1 — Small eigengaps can reduce oracle accuracy.** At fixed $n$ and $t$, sufficiently small gaps are expected to make $B$ less accurate. Strict monotonicity is not assumed; any nonmonotone behavior will be reported.

**H2 — Oracle accuracy does not guarantee plug-in accuracy.** Even when $B$ is close to $R$, $\widehat B$ may be highly variable or may substantially underestimate risk. The two benchmarks may become unreliable in different regions.

**Secondary questions.** Does $t$ affect reliability at fixed $n$ and $\delta$? Can the sample boundary gap or $\widehat B$ indicate a risk of underprediction? Can typical plug-in estimates rank configurations by expected risk?

These hypotheses apply to the stated Gaussian model. The proposed accuracy thresholds are descriptive choices, not universal phase-transition boundaries.

## 5. Measurements and comparisons

The two primary tests have distinct purposes:

| Test | Comparison | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **E3-A: Oracle accuracy** | $B$ against $R$ | Evaluate the first-order approximation |
| **E3-B: Plug-in accuracy** | $\widehat B$ against $R$ | Evaluate the sample-based risk estimate |
| **Secondary analyses** | Spread, ranking, realized $L_k$ | Assess practical limitations and additional behavior |

For configuration $c=(n,\delta,t)$, the oracle benchmark and risk are written as $B_c$ and $R_c$. Replication $r$ produces $L_c^{(r)}$, $\widehat B_c^{(r)}$, and the sample boundary gap $\widehat\delta_{3,c}^{(r)}$.

### 5.1 Estimating population risk

For $M$ independent replications, the Monte Carlo estimate is

$$
\widehat R_c=\frac1M\sum_{r=1}^{M}L_c^{(r)},
\qquad
\operatorname{MCSE}(\widehat R_c)=\frac{s_{L,c}}{\sqrt M}.
$$

A 95% Monte Carlo confidence interval will be reported for each $R_c$. A configuration will be marked **uncertain** if sampling error prevents a clear decision about the chosen accuracy threshold.

### 5.2 E3-A: Oracle accuracy

Three discrepancies are calculated for each configuration:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Signed error:}\quad&D_{\mathrm{signed},c}=B_c-\widehat R_c,\\
\text{Absolute error:}\quad&D_{\mathrm{abs},c}=|B_c-\widehat R_c|,\\
\text{Relative error:}\quad&D_{\mathrm{rel},c}=\frac{|B_c-\widehat R_c|}{\widehat R_c}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Primary output:** Continuous error maps across sample sizes, eigengaps, and spectral families.

For an optional **within-tolerance** label, the rule is

$$
|B_c-R_c|\le\max\{0.002,\ 0.20R_c\}.
$$

The label will be assigned using $\widehat R_c$ and its confidence interval. If Monte Carlo uncertainty could change the label, the configuration will be marked **uncertain**. The absolute tolerance of 0.002 and relative tolerance of 20% are study-specific choices, not industry standards.

### 5.3 E3-B: Plug-in accuracy

The target is **population risk $R_c$**, not the loss of a particular sample. The main quantity is the distribution of $\widehat B_c^{(r)}-R_c$, with $\widehat R_c$ used as the Monte Carlo reference.

Four aspects are evaluated for each configuration:

1. **Typical error.** The median of $\widehat B_c$, its signed difference from $\widehat R_c$, and $\operatorname{median}(|\widehat B_c-\widehat R_c|)$ are reported. The last quantity is also divided by $\widehat R_c$ to give a relative error measure.
2. **Spread and extreme values.** The 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th, and 99th percentiles of raw $\widehat B_c$ are reported, along with the fraction exceeding 1 and any non-finite numerical results.
3. **Serious underprediction.** For configurations with $R_c\ge0.10$, the frequency
   $$
   q_c=\Pr(\widehat B_c\le0.05)
   $$
   is estimated with a confidence interval. The condition $R_c\ge0.10$ is treated as established only if the confidence interval for $R_c$ lies entirely above 0.10. Otherwise, the classification remains **uncertain**. The relative underprediction frequency $\Pr(\widehat B_c<0.5R_c)$ is also reported.
4. **Capped estimate.** Mean absolute error and RMSE are calculated for $\widehat B_{\mathrm{cap}}$ relative to $R_c$. These measures are appropriate for the bounded estimate, but not for raw $\widehat B$.

**Primary output:** A map of median relative absolute plug-in error, displayed alongside the oracle error map. Separate plots will show prediction spread and underprediction frequency. A small median error does not rule out misleading estimates in individual samples.

The two sources of error are separated by the identity

$$
\underbrace{\widehat B_c-R_c}_{\text{total plug-in error}}
=
\underbrace{(\widehat B_c-B_c)}_{\text{error from sample eigenvalues}}
+
\underbrace{(B_c-R_c)}_{\text{oracle approximation error}}.
$$

Robust summaries of $\widehat B_c-B_c$ will be reported together with the oracle error. Agreement caused by cancellation between the two errors **will not be treated as evidence that the theory is accurate**.

### 5.4 Secondary analyses

- **Risk ranking.** For each fixed $(n,t)$, the seven median plug-in estimates are compared with the seven corresponding population-risk estimates. Spearman correlation may be used. Each comparison contains only seven configurations, so the results require cautious interpretation.
- **Calibration of capped estimates.** For each $(n,t)$, observations from the seven eigengaps are pooled and divided into five groups based on quantiles of $\widehat B_{\mathrm{cap}}$. Within each group, the mean prediction is compared with the mean reference risk of its observations. Group sizes and uncertainty intervals will be reported. Groups affected by ties at the cap will be merged. This evaluates the capped estimator, not the raw estimator.
- **Realized loss (exploratory).** The relationship between $\widehat B$ and the loss $L_k$ from the same sample will be examined separately. This differs from estimating population risk $R$.

### 5.5 Describing the transition region

The primary findings will be presented as continuous error maps. For descriptive labels, the same tolerance is used for both benchmarks:

$$
T_c=\max\{0.002,\ 0.20R_c\}.
$$

The oracle is **within tolerance** if $|B_c-R_c|\le T_c$. The plug-in estimate is **typically within tolerance** if

$$
\operatorname{median}\big(|\widehat B_c-R_c|\big)\le T_c.
$$

The median describes typical accuracy, not reliability for every sample. Plug-in spread and underprediction frequency remain separate results. Both criteria use the Monte Carlo estimate $\widehat R_c$. If uncertainty could change a label, that configuration is marked **uncertain**.

Three descriptive categories may be used:

1. **Both typically accurate:** Both benchmark criteria are satisfied.
2. **Oracle accurate, plug-in typically inaccurate:** Only the oracle criterion is satisfied.
3. **Oracle inaccurate:** The oracle criterion is not satisfied, regardless of apparent plug-in accuracy.

These labels depend on the stated tolerance and do not define universal boundaries. Uncertainty and agreement caused by error cancellation will be reported. A classification of typical plug-in accuracy does not imply that large individual errors are absent.

## 6. Simulation plan and validation

**Pilot:** The pilot consists of 1,000 replications per configuration. Its purpose is to check implementation, numerical stability, runtime, and prediction distributions. Pilot results are exploratory.

**Confirmation:** The confirmatory stage consists of 5,000 **new** replications per configuration, using seeds separate from those of the pilot. The analysis plan will be frozen before confirmatory results are examined. Matched $(L,\widehat B,\widehat\delta_3)$ values, or equivalent lossless records, will be retained.

Under the Gaussian model,

$$
(n-1)\widehat\Sigma\sim W_d(\Sigma,n-1).
$$

Direct Wishart simulation may be used for efficiency after its results have been checked against centered Gaussian sampling.

Uncertainty will be summarized with 95% intervals for means, quantiles, and event frequencies. Bootstrap resampling will preserve complete replication records whenever both estimated risk and plug-in values are involved. Comparisons between $t=0$ and $t=0.8$ will use matched $(n,\delta)$ conditions, with confidence intervals that account for any shared-random-number design.

Configurations that remain ambiguous after 5,000 replications will be labeled **uncertain**. Additional simulations informed by confirmatory results will be identified as follow-up analysis. Each figure will be linked to its seeds, parameters, code revision, software versions, and simulation run.

### Tests required before confirmation

The implementation must pass the following checks:

1. Population and sample projectors are symmetric, idempotent, and have rank $k$, within numerical tolerance.
2. Projector loss lies in $[0,1]$, equals zero for identical subspaces, and is invariant under sign changes and basis rotations within a subspace.
3. Principal-angle and projector-based formulas give the same loss in small test cases.
4. The oracle formula agrees with hand-calculated examples and uses the same $n-1$ convention as sample covariance.
5. Population spectra are positive, correctly ordered, and have the intended boundary gap $\delta_3=\delta$.
6. Gaussian and Wishart simulations agree on selected covariance moments and risk estimates.
7. Raw $\widehat B$ is neither silently capped nor modified by an arbitrary eigengap floor. Non-finite values and numerical overflows are recorded and investigated.
8. Interval and bootstrap procedures handle capped-estimate ties, heavy tails, and matched records correctly.

## 7. Deliverables and limitations

**Deliverables:** Oracle signed, absolute, and relative error maps; plug-in error maps; prediction quantiles and underprediction frequencies; a comparison of the two error sources; secondary calibration and ranking results; uncertainty intervals; tests; configuration files; simulation manifests; and reproducibility instructions.

**Interpretation limits:**

- Agreement with established first-order theory in simulations does not constitute a new theorem.
- Oracle accuracy under Gaussian sampling does not establish accuracy for equity returns.
- A small typical plug-in error does not rule out serious underprediction in individual samples.
- Capping can improve numerical accuracy for a bounded target but does not repair the underlying approximation.
- A finite Monte Carlo mean of raw $\widehat B$ does not imply a finite mathematical mean.
- Results for $d=12$ and $k=3$ do not establish universal or dimension-free thresholds.
- Analyses added after the results are examined will be labeled exploratory unless tested independently.

**Later robustness work:** Selected findings will be checked at another $d$ and/or $k$. This will remain separate from the primary 84-configuration experiment. The original hypotheses will not be rewritten after results are observed.

## 8. Results and conclusions

**Not yet run.** This protocol contains no empirical results, measured reliability regions, or conclusions.

After confirmation, the report will include numerical results, uncertainty estimates, comparisons with the hypotheses, robustness checks, and limitations. Ambiguous and unfavorable findings will be reported alongside favorable findings.
